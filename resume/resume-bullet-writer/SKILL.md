# Resume Bullet Writer

Adapted from the resume-bullet-writer skill in github.com/paramchoudhary/resumeskills (MIT license). The original tells you to estimate numbers when real ones are missing and to put a metric in every bullet. Both instructions are removed here on purpose. They conflict with Kirti's rule against invented metrics, and metric stuffing is what made her November 2026 resume unbelievable to readers.

## When to use

* She asks to write, fix, strengthen, shorten, or audit resume bullets or a summary.
* You are about to hand her any resume wording, even a single bullet.

## Sources of truth

Read these before writing. Use only facts found in them or confirmed by her in the conversation.

* Project doc "experience bank"
* Project file Kirti_Gupta_Master_Resume_Clean.pdf, which holds her calibrated verbs for each role
* The project instructions on resume tailoring

If a fact is in neither place, ask her. Never estimate, round up, or infer a number.

## Bullet frameworks

Pick whichever fits the accomplishment.

* X Y Z: accomplished X, as measured by Y, by doing Z.
* CAR: challenge, action, result.
* Her house order: action, then scope, then impact. Results come before mechanism.

## Credibility rules

These matter more than the frameworks.

1. At most two or three numbers in one bullet. If a bullet has more, keep her own impact numbers and cut context numbers first (client revenue, a customer's user count).
2. Each number appears once on the page. Do not repeat a metric in both Summary and Experience unless she asks.
3. Match the verb to her actual ownership. Check the master resume. If the master says "worked on", "conducted", or "contributed to", do not upgrade it to "owned", "redesigned", "engineered", or "secured".
4. Team or business results are attributed honestly: "supporting", "contributing to", "during period of". Never present a business outcome as solely hers.
5. Seniority check: read the bullet next to the job title. If an Analyst or Associate bullet sounds like a partner wrote it, tone it down.
6. Interview test: she must be able to explain every number and every technical term in a live interview. If unsure, ask her.

## Language rules

* Professional verbs only. Never use casual verbs such as spotted, flagged, kicked off, dug into, or nailed.
* Good verbs: led, built, developed, owned (only when true), designed, implemented, conducted, identified, delivered, presented, analyzed.
* No articles (a, an, the) in bullets. Summary may use them.
* No dashes of any kind. Write "go to market", "follow on", "rules based".
* No AI writing patterns. Run the humanizer skill on the summary.

## Shape rules

* Aim for two full lines per bullet. Never three or more.
* No one or two word dangling last line.
* AWS full time role: six bullets at most.
* Summary: three or four complete sentences that flow, leading with AWS. No fragments, no list of percentages, no "leader" unless the title supports it. Do not mention financial services unless needed.

## Output format

For each bullet you change, give:

1. Current wording
2. The specific problem (accuracy, credibility, clarity, tone, length)
3. Proposed wording
4. What is lost, if anything

If a bullet is already accurate, professional, and concise, say "keep it". Do not offer cosmetic alternatives.

## Final check before showing her

* Count the numbers on the whole page. Above roughly 25, look for cuts.
* Every claim traced to the experience bank, master resume, or her confirmation.
* No casual verbs, no dashes, no articles in bullets.
* Give wording in chat. Do not build a document or PDF until she asks.
