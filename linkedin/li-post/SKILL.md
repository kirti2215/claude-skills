# li-post

Adapted from Jake Schincariol's linkedin-agent-skill (MIT). Turns one raw idea into a LinkedIn post that sounds like Kirti.

## Before you write

1. Use Kirti's own voice: analytical, conversational, technically credible, grounded in real work. If you are unsure of her voice, ask for two or three of her past posts or Strategy Lens articles and infer it from those. Do not invent a voice.
2. If the idea is thin ("post about AI"), do not pad it. Ask one batched question: what happened, to whom, and what it cost or returned. A post needs one specific true thing.
3. Never make unemployment, visa status or application counts the subject of a post unless she explicitly asks.

## The shape

LinkedIn rewards dwell time, saves and comments, in that order.

* Line 1: the hook, alone. It must survive truncation at about 140 characters on mobile.
* Line 2: the payoff of line 1, not setup for line 3.
* Body: short paragraphs of one to three lines, blank line between each.
* The turn: one line that reframes what came before.
* Close: one specific question or one instruction. Never both.

Length: 900 to 1,300 characters for a text post. Under 400 reads as a thought. Over 2,000 has to earn every line.

## The loop

1. Pick three hooks from the list below, using different formulas. Show them as three numbered lines and say which you would ship and why, in one sentence.
2. Draft the full post on the strongest hook.
3. Run the draft through the humanizer skill before showing it. No dashes of any kind.
4. Print the post in a fenced block, exactly as it should be pasted, then a short receipt: hook used, character count, and where any link should go.
5. Never publish. Kirti posts it herself.

## Rules

* One idea per post. If the draft has two, say so and split it.
* Numbers over adjectives. If a number is needed and unknown, leave {{your number}} and flag it. Never invent metrics, clients or outcomes.
* No engagement bait ("Thoughts?", "Agree?"). The closing question must be one only this post could ask.
* Three hashtags maximum, at the bottom, only real categories.
* No links in the post body. Put the link in the first comment and say so.

## The 21 hook formulas

Format: name. Template. Best for. Trap to avoid.

1. Contrarian Take. "Everyone says {common advice}. After {specific experience}, I think that is wrong." Best for a point of view; highest comment rate. Trap: being contrarian about something nobody believes.
2. Number Reveal. "I {did thing} for {N} {days/weeks}. Here is what actually happened." Best for experiments with a countable result. Trap: burying the number; it belongs in line 1.
3. Mistake Confession. "{Specific cost} is what {one mistake} cost me." Best for trust. Trap: a fake humble mistake that reads as a brag.
4. Before and After. "{Time ago} I was {low state}. Today I {high state}. The difference was {one thing}." Best for proof you travelled the distance. Trap: naming several levers; name one.
5. The List Promise. "{N} things I wish someone had told me before {milestone}." Best for saves. Trap: N above 10.
6. Insider Secret. "After {N} years {doing thing}, here is the part nobody puts in the {job posting/course/pitch}." Best for credentialed takes; years must be real. Trap: delivering common knowledge.
7. The Callout. "If you are still {doing outdated thing}, stop." Best for short single instruction posts. Trap: contempt for your own audience.
8. Question Trap. "{Specific scenario with a real cost}. What do you do?" Best for comment volume. Trap: a question with an obvious answer.
9. Story Cold Open. A real line of dialogue, then who said it, when, and why it mattered. Best for dwell time. Trap: dialogue nobody would actually say.
10. The Receipt. "{Hard number}. {One sentence of context}." Best for anything provable; pair with an image. Trap: a number with no story.
11. Myth Bust. "{Popular explanation} is not why {bad outcome} is happening." Best for reframing. Trap: never naming the real cause.
12. The Comparison. "{Expensive option} vs {cheap option}. {Surprising verdict}." Best for tool and process posts. Trap: an unfair comparison; concede what the expensive option does better.
13. Permission Slip. "You are allowed to {thing the reader feels guilty about}." Best for reach beyond the niche. Trap: generic self care.
14. Pattern Interrupt. A one to three word line, full stop. Best for stopping the scroll. Trap: overuse; once a month at most.
15. The Warning. "{Common practice} is costing you {specific thing}." Best for audiences who have not linked cause to cost. Trap: fear with no fix.
16. Good vs Great. "Good {role} {do X}. Great {role} {do Y}." Best for quotable lines. Trap: Y is just more effort instead of a different action.
17. Time Anchor. "{Task} used to take me {long time}. It now takes {short time}." Best for tool, system and automation posts. Trap: an unbelievable ratio.
18. The Unpopular Rule. "I do not {widely accepted practice}. Ever. Here is why." Best for positioning. Trap: a rule you actually break.
19. Curiosity Gap. "The {superlative} {thing} I ever {verb} {broke the obvious rule}." Best for forcing the see more tap. Trap: never closing the gap by line 4.
20. The Walk Away. "I {quit/cancelled/dropped} {valuable thing}. {Result}." Best for values posts with a result. Trap: no named cost.
21. The Direct Value. "Here is the exact {artifact} I used to {specific outcome}. Steal it." Best for giving away a template or workflow. Trap: making readers ask for something you could have posted.
