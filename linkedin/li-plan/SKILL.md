# li-plan

Adapted from Jake Schincariol's linkedin-agent-skill (MIT). This decides what gets written; li-post writes it. Run once a week.

## Input

Ask for four things if they are not already known:

1. The audience she wants to reach (for example product marketing and go to market leaders, security and AI founders, technical recruiters).
2. The three or four themes she wants to be known for.
3. What actually happened this week: something she built, a number, a mistake, a conversation, an article she published. Posts come from this.
4. Ten to twenty people or companies worth being visible to.

Do not repeat a theme from the last two weeks.

## What to post

Two to three strong posts a week beats daily posting. Never two of the same type back to back.

* Proof (1 per week): something that happened, with a number.
* Opinion (1 per week): a position someone could disagree with.
* Teach (1 per week): one thing the reader can do today.
* Story (1 every two weeks): a real scene with a cost.
* Build (1 every two weeks): something she built, with how it works and what it changed.

For each slot give the theme, the specific angle drawn from this week, and the hook formula number from li-post. An angle, not a topic. "AI" is not a plan.

Keep unemployment, visa status and application counts out of the plan unless she explicitly asks.

## When to post

For a US B2B audience, Tuesday to Thursday, 7:30 to 9:30am in the audience's time zone, is the working default. Say plainly that the first line matters far more than the hour.

## Who to engage with

A list of 10:

* 5 reach: people with an audience she wants, whose posts she can genuinely add to. Comment early.
* 3 peers: same level and field. This group reciprocates.
* 2 hiring side: leaders or recruiters at target companies. Comment thoughtfully for weeks before any DM.

Twenty minutes a day, before posting.

## Output

A day by day plan for the week (day, time, post type, hook number, angle), then the engagement list. End with: say "write Tuesday" and I will draft it. Nothing is scheduled or posted; Kirti runs the plan. Humanize all text and use no dashes.
