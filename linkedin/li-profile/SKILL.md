# li-profile

Adapted from Jake Schincariol's linkedin-agent-skill (MIT), with the rubric changed for a job seeker. A recruiter decides in a few seconds, from the headline and the first two lines of About, whether to keep reading. LinkedIn Recruiter search leans on headline, titles, skills and Open to Work settings.

## Input

Ask her to paste the headline, About, the last two or three roles, and whether she has a banner, Featured items and Open to Work set. A LinkedIn profile PDF or data export also works. Do not log into LinkedIn for her.

## The rubric (100 points)

1. Headline (12): includes the target role words recruiters search for (for example Product Marketing, GTM), her domains (cybersecurity, cloud, AI), and one piece of proof (former AWS, former security engineer). Reads as a positioning line, not a keyword dump.
2. About, first two lines (10): everything before "see more" says who she is and what she does. First person. No "passionate about", no third person bio.
3. About body (10): first person, concrete proof with numbers, her search keywords appearing naturally in sentences, an optional short keyword block at the end. Ideally 1,300 to 1,800 characters.
4. Featured (8): three or more items, including her strongest proof asset (GitHub work, a Strategy Lens article) and recent work. Nothing stale.
5. Banner (4): one line of positioning, not a default or stock image.
6. Photo (6): face fills most of the frame, recent, clean background.
7. Most recent role (10): one line of scope, then outcome bullets with numbers, following what she built, what problem it solved, and what impact it had. Dates match her resume exactly.
8. Earlier experience (8): earlier roles keep meaningful bullets where they support her positioning (Deloitte and Maersk carry her security story, so do not cut them to one line). Promotions shown as separate titles.
9. Skills (8): top three pinned skills are the ones she wants to be hired for; about 50 relevant skills, no filler like Microsoft Word or Windows.
10. Recommendations (8): at least three, naming specific outcomes, ideally covering both GTM work and security work.
11. Activity (8): posted or commented in the last seven days.
12. Recruiter settings and contact (8): Open to Work set to recruiters only with the right titles and locations, custom URL, working contact info, consistent titles and dates with her resume.

Score every item honestly, show the table and the total. Most first pass profiles land in the 30s to 50s.

## Rewrite in fix first order

Fix the biggest point losses first, one section at a time, so she can paste each in. Never invent achievements, inflate ownership, or change official titles. Use only verified metrics. Keep the technical, security and commercial story together.

## Output

Score table, then copy ready rewrites in fix first order, each run through the humanizer skill with no dashes. Re score at the end and state honestly what the remaining points need (usually recommendations, activity history and a banner, which no rewrite can create). Nothing is saved to LinkedIn by this skill.
